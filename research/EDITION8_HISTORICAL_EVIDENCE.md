# MANTICORE JORJANI ATLAS — EDITION 8: HISTORICAL EVIDENCE AUDIT

**Review date:** 2026-10-29. **Scope:** Six historical-source dossiers, archival comparisons, and 90 contextually normalized but audio-unverified names. This is an audit of primary documentary support, not an endorsement of extraordinary claims.

## Key archival findings

**Carolingian texts identified independently.** *Admonitio generalis* of 23 March 789, chapter 65, names and forbids *tempestarii* (weather-workers), augury, and incantation. It does **not** command the reporting of flying beings. Agobard of Lyon’s *Liber de grandine et tonitruis* chapter II describes the popular *Magonia* cloud-ship belief and people accused of descending from such ships, but he treats the belief as error. The academic repertory identifies the surviving witness as **Paris, BnF Latin 2853 folios 93r–106r** (ninth century). This is a substantive positive match to medieval textual history **and a negative match** to the claimed UFO edict.

**1947 Schulgen facsimile.** The reproduced Army intelligence document’s §4 uses a **Russian-origin manned-aircraft assumption** based on German developments, and §§5–6 request information about Horten designs. The image does **not** say an alien vehicle had been recovered. The September 23 Twining opinion and October Schulgen document are different items. The exact later document copy discussed by the speaker has not been furnished.

**1971 Australian signed original.** National Archives of Australia, **A13693 / 3092/2/000 / barcode 30030606**, contains the May 27, 1971 signed O. H. Turner memorandum (PDF p.7) discussing UFO intelligence hypotheses. The file includes official skepticism about devoting scarce resources. The report does not establish the alleged Agnew Bahnson CIA role.

**1984 Puharich self-citation.** A reproduced paper attributed to Puharich and dated 12 August 1984 references an unpublished manuscript **“TMT, The Magnetic Threat”**. The RT17 page advertises a large downloadable work under the later title *Tesla's Magnifying Transmitter — Electromagnetic Warfare*. Its binary was not obtained or independently compared; its technical hypotheses are not thereby validated.

**Vatican Magenta case.** A Vatican Apostolic Archive official publicly disputed the claim of a Vatican UFO dossier. The purported Italian RS/33 telegrams entered circulation anonymously; an archival chain of custody and an actual Vatican item number remain missing. Distinguish stated archival position from proof that no undiscovered record could exist.

**Stephenson–Brown telegram.** Repeated biography and family accounts exist, but none of the consulted material supplies a dated original telegram from William Stephenson to Josephine Brown. Another Navy separation message from 1942 must not be conflated with this story.

## Exact finding register

### E8-01 — 1971 Australian intelligence memorandum and Bahnson allegation

**Verdict:** PRIMARY DOCUMENT INSPECTED / CLAIM UNCORROBORATED

**Citation:** National Archives of Australia, A13693, 3092/2/000, barcode 30030606, 59 pages; Department of Defence JIO minute, 27 May 1971, signed O. H. Turner (PDF p.7).

- The signed 27 May 1971 JIO minute titled Scientific and Intelligence Aspects of the UFO Problem is present on PDF page 7, surrounded by actual government file correspondence.
- The assessment discusses reported UFO phenomena and possibilities for intelligence analysis, not a confirmed U.S. antigravity program.
- Earlier official correspondence in the same file argues against diverting limited Australian scientific-intelligence resources to UFO reports.
- An indexed search and inspection did not identify Agnew Bahnson as a participant, intermediary, or CIA cutout in the document. The episode sentence has an ambiguous pronoun.

**Not established:** The claimed Bahnson / CIA cutout relationship, or that this officer’s speculative assertions were accepted Australian government policy.

**Required evidence:** The exact speaker passage disambiguating the pronoun plus a contemporary document directly naming Bahnson in the asserted role.

**Sources:** https://recordsearch.naa.gov.au/SearchNRetrieve/Interface/ViewImage.aspx?B=30030606&S=1, https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/australia/A13693_3092-2-000_30030606.pdf

### E8-02 — Carolingian weather-magic law versus Agobard’s Magonia account

**Verdict:** MEDIEVAL TEXTS IDENTIFIED / AERIAL EDICT NOT FOUND

**Citation:** Charlemagne, Admonitio generalis (23 March 789), chapter 65; Agobard of Lyon, Liber de grandine et tonitruis, ch. 2, manuscript Paris, BnF Latin 2853 fols. 93r–106r (9th century).

- Charlemagne’s Admonitio generalis, ch.65, explicitly forbids augury, magical incantation, and tempestarii (storm-workers); it does not command reports about visitors from the air.
- Agobard of Lyon (written ca. 815–817 according to the German medieval-source repertory, with scholarly dating alternatives) reports a belief in Magonia and ships sailing through clouds.
- Agobard describes four accused people (three men, one woman) brought before a crowd as persons believed to have descended from such ships; Agobard disputes the belief as foolish and describes persuading the accusers.
- This is first-rate documentary evidence of a medieval belief and accusation, not evidence that sky-ships were physically present or an edict mandated aerial reporting.
- The sole surviving 9th-century manuscript of Agobard’s tract is BnF Ms. lat. 2853, folios 93r–106r. The digital manuscript locator is catalogued; individual folio images were not retrieved in this environment.

**Not established:** A medieval order about alien visitors or the literal existence of Magonia; later esoteric paraphrases should not be treated as Charlemagne’s text.

**Required evidence:** A manuscript or critical capitulary edition with an explicit aerial-reporting mandate; the extant ch.65 instead covers magical/weather-making practices.

**Sources:** https://lacabalesta.it/biblioteca/MGH/Capitularia/Capitulare022_AdmonitioGeneralis.html, https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/49, https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Liber_de_grandine_et_tonitruis

### E8-03 — Puharich’s complete electromagnetic-warfare manuscript

**Verdict:** MANUSCRIPT PUBLICATION IDENTIFIED / FULL COPY NOT INSPECTED

**Citation:** RT17, Tesla’s Magnifying Transmitter — Electromagnetic Warfare release, download endpoint 11490; Andrija Puharich, invited Tesla Centennial Symposium paper (August 12, 1984), references, item 1.

- RT17 has an item-level release page with title, text description, and an advertised 49.53-MB binary download.
- A 1984 paper attributed to Puharich at the IEEE Pikes Peak Section Tesla Centennial Symposium cites his own then-unpublished manuscript as “TMT, The Magnetic Threat.”
- The publisher says the manuscript includes a second part on proposed countermeasures; an anthology independently reproduces five chapters.
- The 1984 author self-reference supports the existence of a manuscript/work family and a variant title, not scientific validation of scalar-weapons allegations.

**Not established:** That a 49.53-MB file’s entire content has been retrieved, its pagination verified, or its technical claims established experimentally.

**Required evidence:** Acquire an actual complete PDF or physical copy; hash it, verify title pages/edition, compare chapters and passages to the anthology and video.

**Sources:** https://www.rt17.hr/andrija-puharich-teslas-magnifying-transmitter/, https://rexresearch.com/puharelf/puharich.htm, https://www.rt17.hr/download/11490/

### E8-04 — Twining September 1947 opinion / Schulgen October 1947 collection memorandum

**Verdict:** FACSIMILE INSPECTED / ALTERED-VERSION ATTRIBUTION DISPUTED

**Citation:** NARA Record Group 165 archival correspondence included in the 42-page file reproduced in the William Moore collection; October 1947 Schulgen collection memorandum, §4–6, PDF p.7; official NARA transfer letter dated 25 Aug 1983.

- The 42-page collection reproduces National Archives and Records Service correspondence from 1983–1984 about Army intelligence file 452.1 on unconventional aircraft.
- The collection memorandum §4 explicitly assumes a manned Russian-origin craft developed from German aviation technology for analytic purposes.
- The next sections request investigation of Horten flying-wing work; later technical question lists are requests for information, not recovered-device measurements.
- This October memorandum is not the September 23 Twining AMC opinion. Later circulations with “interplanetary craft” wording are not attested by the reproduced memo; the copy held by the interview speaker remains unlocated.

**Not established:** That the 1947 U.S. investigation found alien vehicles, zero-point propulsion, or hardware based on Horten craft.

**Required evidence:** Acquire the exact scan or printout the speaker is discussing and conduct a line-by-line variant comparison against the archival facsimile and known later edits.

**Sources:** https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/SHULGEN-GEN-GEO-F_COLLECTION-MEMO-1947_RE-HORTON-BROS-ETC.pdf, https://www.project1947.com/fig/47docdex.htm

### E8-05 — Vatican Magenta transfer dossier / anonymously circulated RS/33 papers

**Verdict:** PUBLIC ALLEGATION LOCATED / NO VERIFIED VATICAN ARCHIVAL ITEM

**Citation:** Catholic News Service, Robert Duncan, 18 June 2024, interview with Marco Grilli (Vatican Apostolic Archive); RS/33 papers anonymously received by investigator Roberto Pinotti in 1996 according to published accounts.

- An account of anonymous purported RS/33 telegrams and a recovery protocol circulates publicly; the papers’ chain of custody starts at anonymous delivery, not a verified 1933 archive accession.
- Paper/ink allegations about period correctness do not establish document authorship or authenticity without accessible forensic reports and custody records.
- Vatican Apostolic Archive representative Marco Grilli disputed claims of UFO-related Vatican documents in a contemporary on-record interview.
- The institutional statement is evidence of the archive’s stated position, not universal proof about every undocumented possible holding.

**Not established:** A 1933 Vatican archival dossier, a 1933 Magenta craft recovery, or any Pius XII/U.S. transfer.

**Required evidence:** Item-level Vatican/Italian/American accession, dated original scans and independent chain-of-custody documentation of the asserted transfer.

**Sources:** https://www.usccb.org/news/2024/angels-or-aliens-some-researchers-say-vatican-archives-hold-ufo-secrets, https://www.uaptrail.com/document/rs33-protocol-1933/

### E8-06 — Alleged William Stephenson → Josephine Brown 1940s telegram

**Verdict:** SECONDHAND NARRATIVE / AUTHENTIC TELEGRAM NOT LOCATED

**Citation:** American Alchemy, Part 3, native-caption 979–991 seconds; Paul Schatzkin, The Man Who Mastered Gravity, biographical investigation of T. Townsend Brown; Brown family timeline.

- The conversation explicitly attributes a telegram reporting Brown injured but safe to Stephenson and Josephine Brown.
- The Townsend Brown biographical and family narratives discuss purported work with Stephenson, but they do not expose a dated original telegram with archival accession in the sources inspected.
- A distinct October 5, 1942 naval separation telegram has been reported in the published biography and must not be confused with the alleged Stephenson-to-Josephine telegram.
- Contradictory details in adjacent Brown 1930s–1940s anecdotes make a 1944/45 secret-mission inference unreliable without original documentation.

**Not established:** The message’s exact words, origin, sender, delivery, 1944/1945 date, or the accompanying covert mission.

**Required evidence:** A legible telegram or recognized archive transcription with provenance, addressee, signature, date, body and chain of custody.

**Sources:** https://thomastownsendbrown.com/misc/timeline.htm, https://www.ttbrown.com/the-man-who-mastered-gravity/intro2023/

## Source policy

- An artifact's *existence* and an assertion’s *truth* are separate propositions.
- The graph counts **distinct claim records**, not independent corroborations, certainty, or causal strength.
- Negative findings from a bounded set of archives do not establish impossibility of an original outside that set.
- The dataset is complete only relative to the audited **known items**. It cannot be declared exhaustively complete for all historical evidence without obtaining the missing originals and reviewing original audio.
